## Understand the analysis before buying the software

Restaurant site selection brings together several questions: who lives nearby, who actually visits, what alternatives customers have, and whether your concept suits the area. Demographic platforms describe populations and spending, while visitation platforms add evidence about where people go. Restaurant-focused tools can organize competitor and concept-fit signals into a more accessible screening report.

Keep those evidence types separate. A neighborhood profile is not a count of potential diners, and visits to nearby businesses are not transactions at your proposed restaurant. Placer.ai explicitly distinguishes resident demographics from visitation data; use that distinction when reading any location report.

For a useful evaluation, ask each provider to demonstrate one address you already understand. Request the data dates, geographic boundaries, calculation methods, and limitations. Then check whether the report helps resolve a real uncertainty rather than simply producing an attractive map.

## Five tools worth considering in 2026

### Restaurant Site Finder: restaurant-focused initial screening

Restaurant Site Finder is built and maintained by Horeca Store. Its documented features include competitor mapping, review sentiment signals, market gap analysis, GO/NO-GO scoring, and concept-specific equipment planning. The tool is positioned to help operators evaluate an address before signing a lease.

Use it to organize an initial investigation. Run candidate addresses through the tool, then investigate the competitors and proposed market gaps yourself. Treat a favorable score as a reason to continue research—not permission to skip fieldwork.

Ask what supports each recommendation, especially when the output suggests an underserved cuisine. Separate the appeal of a suggested concept from your ability to operate it. ### Census Business Builder: demographic and economic context

[Census Business Builder](https://www.census.gov/data/data-tools/cbb.html) provides selected demographic and economic data through an interface designed for business research. Its features include interactive maps, downloadable data, geographic comparisons, ranking charts, and customizable reports. Users can research locations and business types rather than starting with a blank statistical database.

Use it early to compare neighborhood context before paying for more specialized analysis. Choose consistent boundaries, record the dataset years, and examine several relevant characteristics together. Avoid selecting only the variable that makes your preferred address look attractive.

Use these reports to formulate questions for local research. For example, a household profile might support investigating a family-oriented dinner concept, but you should still observe evening activity. Keep population research in its proper role: context for a decision, not a ready-made sales forecast. Esri ArcGIS Business Analyst combines mapping with demographic, consumer spending, and business data. This is a strong fit when your central question is whether two addresses serve meaningfully different customer areas. Before committing, ask for a demonstration using your own shortlist. Check whether you can reproduce the comparison and explain the selected variables. ### Placer.ai: visitation and competitive context

Placer.ai’s site-selection offering compares candidate locations using visits, dwell time, trade-area reach, audience characteristics, and nearby competitors. It also supports analyzing unopened locations through visitation to surrounding properties. The company offers customized subscription packages rather than a single universal price.

Shortlist it when your biggest uncertainty concerns movement and destination patterns. Its restaurant offering includes competitor visitation trends, audience dining preferences, cross-shopping, and site-selection analysis. Those capabilities make it relevant when comparing established dining corridors or assessing nearby activity.

In a demonstration, ask how visits are estimated, what coverage exists around each address, and how the reporting period affects interpretation. Request comparable views for all candidate sites. Do not convert a nearby property’s visitation figure directly into your expected customer count.

### SiteZeus: expansion and sales-transfer analysis

SiteZeus is a mapping and analytics platform aimed at location analysts, franchise owners, and business development teams. Its Impact Analytics feature estimates how a proposed location could shift visits and revenue from nearby stores in an existing portfolio. That feature requires mobile-data coverage on the relevant nearby stores.

Use it for expansion analysis rather than basic first-location screening. Ask whether the proposed analysis can distinguish newly captured business from business transferred out of your other restaurants. That distinction is central to SiteZeus’s documented cannibalization workflow.

Bring representative locations and a specific decision to the sales conversation. Request an explanation of required inputs, validation, and uncertainty before reviewing a forecast. Evaluate the output against your own operating knowledge instead of accepting precision as proof of accuracy.

## How should you choose location analysis software?

Choose location analysis software by identifying the uncertainty that could change your decision, then purchasing the narrowest capability that resolves it. Start with the business question, not a vendor’s feature list. This keeps the comparison focused on a deliverable you can actually use.

For example, write: “We need to compare weekday lunch potential at three addresses.” That is more actionable than “We need the best restaurant analytics.” Ask each provider to demonstrate how its data would inform that exact comparison.

Use this buying checklist:

- **Decision fit:** Request a sample report for your concept, service model, and intended customer journey.
- **Coverage:** Confirm that each candidate address and relevant surrounding businesses can be analyzed.
- **Data transparency:** Ask for collection periods, update dates, sources, and definitions of modeled values.
- **Comparable outputs:** Check that you can apply the same boundaries, variables, and reporting periods across sites.
- **Usability:** Have the person doing the research complete a realistic task during a trial or demonstration.
- **Total commitment:** Request written terms covering access, exports, additional users, onboarding, and cancellation.

Compare the complete cost of obtaining a usable answer, including your team’s time. Avoid buying a broad platform for one report unless its additional capabilities serve a clear purpose. Conversely, do not force a simple screening tool to answer an expansion-modeling question it was not designed to resolve. ## Turn site analysis into a repeatable decision process

Use site analysis tools within a documented workflow rather than collecting unrelated reports. The goal is a comparison another decision-maker can follow, including reasons to reject the most appealing address. Keep evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions visibly separate.

1. **Define the concept first.** Record service style, menu direction, target customer, expected spending, and priority meal periods. Use the same brief when evaluating every address.
2. **Create a comparable shortlist.** Gather proposed occupancy costs, usable space, access details, and known building constraints. Mark missing information instead of guessing.
3. **Match the trade area to the visit.** Decide whether the analysis should emphasize walking, driving, delivery, or destination trips. Walk-time and drive-time areas differ from simple rings.
4. **Investigate demand and alternatives.** Review customer context, nearby restaurant offerings, and relevant activity. Distinguish directly competing concepts from complementary businesses.
5. **Visit during intended trading periods.** Record observations consistently: access, visibility, queues, pickup activity, parking, and the route from nearby demand generators.
6. **Compare operating scenarios.** Estimate required transactions using your own cost and spending assumptions. Keep conservative and stronger-demand cases separate rather than relying on one forecast. Stress-test rent with the [restaurant rent calculator](/restaurant-rent-calculator).
7. **Document the decision.** Write why the site advances, what could invalidate that choice, and what must be confirmed before commitment.

For a hypothetical lunch-focused café, prioritize testing whether weekday activity is accessible during the actual lunch window. For a destination dinner concept, investigate a different journey and time period. These examples illustrate why one universal location score should not replace a concept-specific comparison. ## Common mistakes to avoid when interpreting reports

**Comparing unlike trade areas.** Do not compare a walk-time report for one address with a broad radius report for another without explaining the difference. Keep the boundary method consistent or justify why customer behavior requires different treatment.

**Treating a market gap as established demand.** Use a suggested gap as a hypothesis to investigate. Review menus, pricing, operating hours, customer feedback, and your own observations before deciding what it means. An automated recommendation should create better research questions, not close them.

**Letting a strong score settle unresolved property questions.** Maintain a separate list for building suitability, operating constraints, and proposed lease terms. Assign each unresolved item to someone responsible for verifying it. If a material question remains unanswered, label the site conditional rather than allowing a dashboard score to conceal the uncertainty.
